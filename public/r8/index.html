Three foundations — typeface, palette and layout together

You said the problem is the font, the colours and the layout, not which card shape we pick. You are right, so this round changes those and nothing else. Each foundation below is a complete system, applied to exactly the same page content so you can compare like for like. Nothing is live.

What is wrong with the foundation today

LayerTodayWhy it hurts
TypefaceBaloo 2 for everything A rounded display face doing body text and numbers. Soft terminals, blobby at heavy weight, and its Devanagari reads informal — the wrong register for treating disease
PaletteFive creams plus neon saffron Ivory, apricot, sandal, paper-cream and white are near-identical, so the page reads muddy; then #FF9933 at full strength on large areas is the only thing with any force
LayoutOne centred stack of sections Every section is the same shape and rhythm, so nothing signals importance and there is no composition — only sequence

The three foundations

1 · “Clinic”

Noto Serif Devanagari for anything read as a claim, Mukta for the interface. Near-white paper so ink looks black rather than brown. One deep burnt-orange does the work; bright saffron survives only on the single action button.

Keeps your saffron, but demotes it from a large surface to one button.

2 · “Modern”

One family across the whole site — Anek Devanagari — with weight and size doing every job and no second typeface at all. Anek was drawn for Indian scripts in this decade; its Devanagari is open and even. Deeper amber replaces the neon saffron so large areas stop shouting.

Replaces #FF9933 with a deeper amber #E8730D.

3 · “Ayurvedic”

The visual language the category already speaks in India: deep green for the practice, saffron for the call to act. Martel is a warm Devanagari serif with real weight range; Hind is the clean companion. Green does the serious work, so saffron can stay bright without dominating.

Reopens your round-1 decision of “no green”. Worth seeing before you keep it.
