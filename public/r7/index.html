Making the important thing the loudest thing

Nothing here is live. Every option below is its own page, shown at real phone width and again at desktop width. Labels and explanations are in English; the interface stays in Hindi because that is the real content, and there is an English translation under each one.

What I measured first

Counted in the browser across five live pages and 646 blocks of text. This is the whole of the problem.

WhatNowWhat it means
Text at the heaviest weight (800)39% Two words in every five are at maximum weight, so weight has stopped signalling anything
Text in faded brown rather than ink53% The default reading colour is the faded one. Only 29% is in full ink

1 · The whole site — pick a temperament

Same three rules in both: ink by default, weight reserved for one thing per block, blanks quieter than their neighbours. The difference is how loud the site is.

Direction A

“The prescription” — quiet, ink-dense

Headline 42px on a phone, 60px on a desktop. Colour appears three times on the whole page. Reads like a doctor’s document: more credible, less arresting.

“Big” — large, high contrast

2 · The batch cards — pick one

All three carry the same facts. Today the seven heavy day-discs are the loudest thing on the card while saying only “Mon–Sat”.

Option A

A card each, price led

One card per batch, the fee heaviest, the day-discs replaced by four plain words. Each card keeps its own button.

Open — phone and desktop › Option B

Both batches in one table

The least furniture, and it answers the real question — which of the two suits me — at a glance. Cost: no per-batch button, so one ask sits underneath.

Open — phone and desktop › Option C

A card each, week as small dots

Keeps the shape of the week visible as seven 9px dots instead of heavy discs. A middle path.
