Making the important thing the loudest thing

Nothing here is live. These are boards to look at and choose from. The interface examples stay in Hindi because that is the real content and Devanagari behaves differently from Latin at the same size — but every label and explanation is in English, with a translation under each example.

Counted in the browser across five live pages and 646 blocks of text. This is the whole of what you have been describing.

2 · The three rules I want to apply everywhere

Everything below follows from these.

Rule 1Ink by default

Every word a person actually reads is in full ink. The faded brown is reserved for true metadata — a date, a count, a consent line. Ten uses a page, not fifty.

Reading

Metadata only

Rule 2Weight means rank

The heaviest weight goes to the page title and to the single most important thing in a block — the result, or the price. Nothing else. Target: under 10%, not 39%.

8.2 → 6.5

Everything else reads like this — plain, but in full ink.

And detail sits back like this.

Rule 3Blanks go quiet

A fact you have not given me yet should be quieter than the words around it, not louder. A soft tinted chip instead of a dotted underline.

6 · Two temperaments for the whole site

Same three rules in both. The difference is how loud the site is. Open each at phone width.

Direction A — “The prescription”

Ink on paper. Headline 42px. Colour appears three times on the whole page: one button, one arrow, one dark warning panel. Reads like a doctor’s document.

Trade-off: more credible and calmer, but less arresting — a fast scroller may not stop.

Open Direction A ›

 Direction B — “Big”

Headline 54px, disease names 28px, the result number 64px, and a solid dark band introducing her. Far fewer things per screen, each much larger.

Trade-off: unmissable at arm’s length on a cheap phone, which is your audience — but it edges towards advertising rather than clinic.
